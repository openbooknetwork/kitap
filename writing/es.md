# Spanish

These add to the rules for translating in [README.md](README.md).

- Write the neutral Spanish of Latin America, in words that readers in Spain share: tomar over coger, ustedes with
  its verbs for a plural "you". A man as the direct object takes lo: Los británicos lo capturaron en 1916.
- The text and the interface address the reader with tú: Elige una batalla. A button names its
  action in the infinitive: Comenzar, Ver el mapa.
- A name takes the form of Spanish histories and of the RAE's lists of place names: Estambul, Galípoli, los
  Dardanelos, Tesalónica (entonces Salónica), el Tratado de Lausana. A ruler takes the Spanish name: Solimán el
  Magnífico, Pedro el Grande. Pasha is Pachá after the name: Enver Pachá.
- A link covers the name. An article or a contraction stays outside it: los [Dardanelos](@dardanelles),
  del [Danubio](@danube). An article that belongs to the name keeps its capital, stays inside and does not
  contract: a [El Cairo](@cairo).
- Months, days, nationalities and offices take a small letter: el 26 de agosto, los turcos, el sultán Mehmed II.
  A title or a label capitalizes its first word and its names only: La campaña de Galípoli.
- Historical names take capitals as the RAE writes them: la Primera Guerra Mundial, la batalla de Sakarya,
  el Tratado de Lausana, el Imperio otomano.
- Quotes are «comillas angulares», with “inglesas” inside them and ‘simples’ inside those:
  «El coronel escribió: “Resistan”».
- Numbers of five digits or more group thousands with a no break space: 250 000. Four digits and years stay
  together: 2500 soldados, 1921. Decimals take the point, as the RAE recommends: 1.5 km. A percent sign follows
  a no break space: 30 %.
- Dates read day, month, year, joined by de: 26 de agosto de 1921, 1 de mayo de 1915. A range takes a hyphen:
  1919-1923. Times of day read 10:30.
- Centuries take Roman numerals: el siglo XIX. The 1920s are los años veinte or la década de 1920. An ordinal in
  figures takes a point and a raised letter: el 57.º Regimiento, la 2.ª División.
- An English -ing clause that tells what happened next becomes a second verb joined by y: El ejército cruzó el río
  y tomó la ciudad. A gerund stays for what happens at the same time: Avanzaron cantando.
- An English passive with no actor named becomes a passive with se or an active verb: El tratado se firmó en 1923.
- A part of the body takes the article in place of the English possessive: Cuando corres, el corazón late más
  rápido. Anatomy takes the schoolbook word: aurícula over atrio, rótula over patela.
