# Russian

These add to the rules for translating in [README.md](README.md).

- The text and the interface address the reader as вы, in lower case, with plural imperatives: Листайте стрелками.
- A name in a link takes its case, and the link covers the whole word: в [Анкаре](@ankara), под [Галлиполи](@gallipoli).
- A name takes the form Russian schoolbooks use: Мустафа Кемаль, Ататюрк, Салоники, Дарданеллы. A title follows
  the name with a hyphen: Исмет-паша.
- A Turkish name with no Russian form follows the practical transcription: c is дж, ç is ч, ş is ш, ı is ы, and ğ
  drops out. After a consonant ö is ё and ü is ю. At the start of a word e is э: Джемаль-паша, Чанаккале,
  Кёпрюлю, Эрдоган, Эскишехир.
- Write ё wherever it belongs, as schoolbooks do: её, вёл, Кёпрюлю.
- Quotes are «ёлочки», with „лапки“ inside them. A dash stands between subject and predicate where grammar puts
  one: Салоники — крупнейший порт.
- The name of an event or a state capitalizes its first word and the proper names in it: Первая мировая война,
  Османская империя. Peoples and months stay in lower case: турки, август.
- Numbers group thousands with a space and take a decimal comma: 250 000, 1,5 км. A number of four digits stays
  whole: 1500.
- Dates read day, month in the genitive, year: 26 августа 1921 года. A range takes an en dash: 1919–1923 годы.
  Centuries take Roman numerals: XIX век. A year before Christ takes до н. э.: в 490 году до н. э.
- Half is пол, joined to the noun, with a hyphen before a vowel, л or a capital: полграмма, пол-Европы.
- Times of day read 8:00. «Союзники» for the Allies of the First World War is Антанта, since Russia was one of them.
- Anatomy takes the terms of Russian schoolbooks: кора больших полушарий over кортекс, желудочек, гипофиз,
  щитовидная железа, надпочечники.
