# Japanese

These add to the rules for translating in [README.md](README.md).

- Prose uses です・ます, as public explanations and museum panels do: ムスタファは1881年にテッサロニキで
  生まれます。A title, a label or a marker note ends in a noun: ムスタファ・ケマルの上陸、1919年5月19日. In the
  interface a button is a noun or a plain verb (次へ, 閉じる), and a message uses です・ます.
- A name takes the form Japanese schoolbooks and school atlases use: オスマン帝国, イスタンブール, アタテュルク,
  ガリポリ. A name they lack is written in katakana by 外来語の表記, with ー for a long vowel and バ行 for v, as in
  バイオリン.
- The parts of a foreign name are joined with ・ (U+30FB), and so is a title that follows the name: ムスタファ・ケマル,
  イスメト・パシャ.
- A Chinese or Korean name takes its kanji in Japanese form, and a Korean name gives its Korean reading in brackets
  the first time a page names it: 毛沢東, 北京, 李舜臣（イ・スンシン）, 仁川（インチョン）.
- An event takes the name Japanese schoolbooks give it: 第一次世界大戦, 日露戦争, 朝鮮戦争, 国共内戦, 太平洋戦争.
- A link covers the name, and the particle follows it outside the link: [アンカラ](@ankara)に,
  [ムスタファ・ケマル](@kemal)は.
- Leave out 彼, 彼女, あなた and 私たち where the sentence reads clearly without them, and repeat the name where it
  does not. A plural takes たち or ら only where the sense needs it. Write the active voice where English uses the
  passive and names the actor: ギリシャ軍がイズミルを占領します。
- Punctuation is 、。 with full width （）「」！？. Quotes are 「」, with 『』 inside them, and a book title takes 『』.
  Japanese text touches digits and Latin letters with no space: 1921年, 第57連隊.
- Numbers take half width digits with the counter of their noun: 3隻, 40門, 7歳. Words, idioms, approximate numbers
  and the names of events keep kanji: 一部, 一つ, 数十人, 第一次世界大戦. A number of ten thousand or more takes 万
  or 億: 25万人, 31万5500人. A smaller number of four digits takes a comma: 5,000人.
- Years are Common Era years, and a year before it takes 紀元前: 1921年8月26日, 紀元前221年. A range takes ～
  (U+FF5E): 1919～1923年. Times of day read 午前8時30分.
- Anatomy terms take the form of Japanese school science books, and the Japanese Association of Anatomists' term
  where they have none: 左心室, 大動脈, 脛骨. A word with a kanji outside the 常用漢字表 gives its reading in brackets
  the first time a page uses it: 脛骨（けいこつ）.
