# Italian

These add to the rules for translating in [README.md](README.md).

- The text and the interface address the reader with tu, as Italian sites for children do: Scegli una battaglia.
  A button names its action in the imperative: Inizia, Mostra la mappa.
- The English simple past becomes the passato remoto, with the imperfetto for what was going on, as Italian
  history books tell it: Nel 1915 gli Alleati sbarcarono a Gallipoli.
- A name takes the form of Treccani and Italian schoolbooks: Istanbul, Smirne, Gallipoli, i Dardanelli, Losanna.
  Thessaloniki (then Salonica) is Salonicco alone, since Italian has one name for both. A ruler takes the Italian
  name: Maometto II, Solimano il Magnifico, Pietro il Grande. Pasha is Pascià after the name: Enver Pascià.
- A link covers the name. An articulated preposition or an elided article stays outside it:
  della [Grecia](@greece), dell’[Anatolia](@anatolia), al [Cairo](@cairo). An article that belongs to the name
  stays inside when it stands alone: [Il Cairo](@cairo).
- A d joins a and e only before the same vowel: ad [Ankara](@ankara), a [Istanbul](@istanbul), ed Enver, e İsmet.
- A city takes feminine agreement, whatever its ending: Salonicco fu occupata nel 1912.
- Months, days, offices and modern peoples take a small letter: il 26 agosto, i turchi, il sultano Maometto II.
  An ancient people takes a capital: i Romani. A title or a label capitalizes its first word only:
  La battaglia di Gallipoli.
- A historical name capitalizes its first word, and battaglia stays small: la Prima guerra mondiale,
  l’Impero ottomano, la battaglia di Gallipoli.
- Quotes are « », with “ ” inside them: «Il colonnello scrisse: “Resistete”».
- Numbers group thousands with a dot and take a decimal comma: 250.000, 1,5 km. Years stay together: 1921.
- Dates read day, month, year: 26 agosto 1921. The first day of a month takes the ordinal sign º (U+00BA):
  1º maggio 1915. A range takes an en dash: 1919–1923. Times of day read 8:30.
- Centuries take Roman numerals or the capitalized name of the hundreds: il XIX secolo, l’Ottocento. The 1920s are
  gli anni Venti.
- A part of the body takes the article in place of the English possessive: Quando corri, il cuore batte più in
  fretta. Anatomy takes the schoolbook word: costole over coste, rotula over patella.
