# Turkish

These add to the rules for translating in [README.md](README.md).

- The interface addresses the reader as siz, in the short imperative: Bir hikâye seçin. Oklarla ilerleyin.
- A suffix on a proper name or a number follows a typographic apostrophe: Ankara’ya, 1920’de. A link covers the name
  and its suffix: [Gelibolu’ya](@gallipoli). A suffix on a foreign name follows how the name sounds: Cannes’a,
  Davout’nun.
- A suffix on the name of an institution joins it directly: Büyük Millet Meclisine, İttihat ve Terakki Cemiyetinin.
  So does a plural or a derivational suffix on any name: Türklerin, Bizanslılar, Avrupalı.
- A month with its day or year starts with a capital and takes the apostrophe: 26 Ağustos’ta, Mart 1544’te. A month
  on its own is lower case and takes its suffix directly: temmuzda, haziran sonunda.
- Dates read day, month, year: 26 Ağustos 1921. A range takes an en dash: 1919–1923. MÖ and MS go before the year:
  MÖ 218, MS 724. Times of day read saat 8.00, 13.30’da.
- Numbers group thousands with a dot and take a decimal comma: 250.000, 1,5 km. The percent sign goes before the
  number: %25.
- An ordinal is a digit with a dot: 13. kral, 57. Alay, 10. yüzyıl. A Roman numeral goes before its noun with a dot:
  II. Mehmed, XIV. Louis, III. Kolordu. A decade takes the plural suffix: 1960’larda.
- A title with a name starts with a capital: İsmet Paşa, Enver Paşa’ya, Sultan Abdülhamid. The same word on its own
  is lower case: paşa olan Mustafa Kemal.
- Ottoman names keep the spelling of Turkish histories: II. Mehmed, I. Murad, Yıldırım Bayezid. Arabic and Persian
  names take their Turkish form: Selahaddin Eyyubi, Harun Reşid.
- A name from another alphabet is spelled as Turkish hears it: Puşkin, I. Aleksandr, Homeros. A name with a Turkish
  form takes it: Napolyon, Viyana, Londra, Atina.
- Words stay Turkish where Turkish has the word in common use: karıncık over ventrikül, taarruz over ofansif. The
  circumflex stays where TDK writes it: hikâye, millî, Kâzım.
- A chronicle's writer is a tarihçi: Osmanlı tarihçilerine göre, Thüringenli tarihçi Johannes Rothe. Where modern
  historians appear in the same passage, the old ones are dönemin tarihçileri. A named chronicle is a Tarihi, or a
  Yıllıkları when it records year by year: Macarlar Tarihi, Anglo-Sakson Yıllıkları. A title in its own language stays
  as written: Kronika velmi pěkná, Chronicon Pictum. Kayıt stays for records, such as archives and registers.
- A subject pronoun drops where the verb shows the person: 1881’de Selanik’te doğar.
- Quotes are “ ”, with ‘ ’ inside them. An English dash inside a sentence becomes a comma, a colon or brackets.
