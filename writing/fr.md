# French

These add to the rules for translating in [README.md](README.md).

- Write the French of France. The text and the interface address the reader with tu, as French sites for
  children do: Choisis une bataille. A button names its action in the infinitive: Commencer, Voir la carte.
- The English past becomes the passé composé, with the imparfait for what was going on, as French history is told
  to children: En 1915, les Alliés ont débarqué à Gallipoli.
- A narrow no break space (U+202F) goes before ; ! ? and a no break space (U+00A0) goes before : and inside « ».
  Quotes are « », with “ ” inside them: « Le colonel écrit : “Tenez !” »
- Numbers group thousands with a narrow no break space and take a decimal comma: 250 000, 1,5 km. Years stay
  together: 1921. A percent sign follows a no break space: 30 %.
- Dates read day, month, year: le 26 août 1921, le 1er mai 1915. A range takes an en dash: 1914–1918. Times of day
  read 8 h 30, 10 h.
- Ordinals abbreviate as 1er, 1re, 2e, 3e: le XIXe siècle, François Ier, la 57e division. Centuries take Roman
  numerals. The 1920s are les années 1920.
- Months, days, adjectives of nationality and offices take a small letter, and a people takes a capital:
  le 26 août, les Turcs, l’armée turque, le sultan Mehmed II. A title or a label capitalizes its first word and its
  names only: La bataille des Dardanelles.
- Historical names take capitals as French histories write them: la Première Guerre mondiale, l’Empire ottoman,
  le traité de Lausanne, la bataille de la Somme.
- Capitals keep their accents, and œ stays joined: l’Égypte, l’État, À l’aube, le cœur, l’œsophage.
- A link covers the name. An article, a contraction or an elided d’ or l’ stays outside it: l’[Égypte](@egypt),
  d’[Ankara](@ankara), au [Caire](@cairo). An article that belongs to the name stays inside when it stands alone:
  [Le Caire](@cairo). The apostrophe is ’.
- A name takes the form of French schoolbooks and the Larousse: Istanbul, Gallipoli, les Dardanelles, Thessalonique
  (alors Salonique). A ruler takes the French name: Soliman le Magnifique, Pierre le Grand. Pasha is Pacha after
  the name: Enver Pacha.
- An English dash around an aside becomes commas or parentheses.
- A part of the body takes the article in place of the English possessive: Quand tu cours, le cœur bat plus vite.
  Anatomy takes the schoolbook word: oreillette over atrium, omoplate over scapula, rotule over patella.
